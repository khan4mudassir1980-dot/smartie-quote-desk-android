# N3.1 Stock Photo — plan (Spark revision)

One optional photo per stock item, stored in Firestore, on the **Spark** plan.
Proposed, not built: nothing here is implemented, no dependency has been
added, and no Firebase project has been touched.

**The decision that shapes this document:** SMARTIE stays on Firebase Spark.
No billing account, no Blaze upgrade, no Cloud Storage, no Cloud Functions, no
paid service. The previous revision of this plan assumed Cloud Storage and is
withdrawn — Cloud Storage requires Blaze even for a default bucket, so it is
not available and its design is not a fallback to keep in reserve.

N3.1 sits on top of N3, which is **open** — single-device staging
verification passed, physical two-device concurrency verification pending
(`docs/N3-verification.md`). N3.1 changes no settled N3 behaviour.

## Verdict on the proposed design

**It is suitable, and it is the right shape for Spark.** Two of its choices
are not merely tidy — they are forced by limits that would otherwise break the
feature outright, and the proposal gets both right. It needs three
refinements, and it carries four limitations that must be accepted with open
eyes rather than discovered later.

I am **not** recommending that photos be postponed. The conditions under which
I would are named at the end.

### The three hard facts this design has to live with

| Fact | Verified | What it forces |
|---|---|---|
| A Firestore document may not exceed **1 MiB − 4 bytes** | [Storage size calculations](https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/storage-size), [Usage and limits](https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/quotas) | An 80 KB photo fits with room to spare. The ceiling is not the problem |
| **The mobile SDKs have no `select()` projection** — it exists only in the server SDKs | [Firestore client libraries](https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/client/libraries) | Android **cannot** fetch a document without one of its fields. A photo on the `/stock` document would be downloaded by every device on every board read, with no way to opt out. **The separate photo document is mandatory, not a preference** |
| An index entry may not exceed **7.5 KiB**; committing a document that breaks it raises **InvalidArgument (2)** | [Index types](https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/query-data/index-overview), [Usage and limits](https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/quotas) | Firestore indexes every field automatically. An 80 KB `bytes` field is ten times the index-entry ceiling, so **every photo write fails** until the field is exempted from indexing. The exemption is a prerequisite, not an optimisation |

That third fact is the one that would have blown up in the first manual test
with an error that looks nothing like its cause. It is handled in batch A, and
the exemption ships and deploys **before** any code that writes a photo.

### The three refinements

1. **Put a marker on the stock row, not just a photo document.** Without one,
   the app cannot tell which rows have a photo without reading every photo
   document — 403 reads to discover that 200 rows have pictures. The board
   already downloads every `/stock` document, so two tiny fields there
   (`hasPhoto`, `photoRev`) are free, and they turn "which rows have photos"
   into information the app already holds.
2. **`photoRev` is the cache key.** A replaced photo must invalidate what is
   cached on every other device. A revision counter on both documents does
   that and doubles as the concurrency mechanism.
3. **Fetch photo bytes lazily, cache-first, per row scrolled into view** —
   never a bulk prefetch. This is what keeps photos off the shared daily
   quota; see the usage section.

### The four limitations, stated plainly

1. **Photos share one daily quota with the whole app.** There is no separate
   photo budget. A day that burns reads on photos is a day with fewer reads
   for stock, products and quotations.
2. **Dimensions cannot be enforced by rules.** Security rules can check the
   byte size of a `bytes` field; they cannot decode an image. 800×800 is a
   client-side promise only, and a modified client could store an 80 KB image
   of any dimensions. The byte ceiling is the enforceable half.
3. **80 KB at 800×800 will not reliably read small printed text.** See "Image
   quality" below. It identifies an item; it is not a document scanner.
4. **Photo bytes compete for the on-device Firestore cache**, which has a
   size limit and evicts least-recently-used data. Photos can push other
   cached data out, and evicted data costs reads to fetch again. Cloud
   Storage would have kept image bytes out of that cache entirely; on Spark
   this is part of the price.

## The user flow

Unchanged from the previous revision, because none of it depended on where
the bytes live.

A stock card with no photo shows a small camera affordance to anyone who may
manage photos. Tapping it offers exactly two options — **Take a photo** and
**Choose from gallery** — the same two-mode shape Add stock uses, because the
first staging pass proved a single crowded sheet is what people get wrong.
Either route lands on a **preview** with **Add photo** and **Cancel**. Nothing
is written until Add photo is pressed.

A card with a photo shows a thumbnail; tapping it opens a larger view. A
Worker gets the thumbnail and the larger view and nothing else. The larger
view carries **Replace** and **Remove** for anyone who may manage photos.

Offline, every photo control is disabled with the wording the stock controls
already use, and a photo already cached on the device stays viewable.

## Proposed defaults

| Default | How it is met |
|---|---|
| One compressed WebP photo per stock item, ≤ 80 KB, ≤ 800×800 | A `bytes` field in a photo document. 80 KB is enforced by rules; 800×800 is enforced by the client only |
| Stored in a separate photo document, never in the stock record | `/stockPhotos/{stockDocId}` — forced by the absence of `select()` in the mobile SDKs |
| Image data excluded from indexing | A `fieldOverrides` entry in `firestore.indexes.json` disabling indexing on the `bytes` field. Mandatory: without it every write fails |
| Fetched and cached individually, only when needed | Lazy, cache-first reads per row scrolled into view. `hasPhoto` on the stock row means no read is spent discovering there is nothing to fetch |
| Immutable stock identity | The photo document id is `Keys.stockDocId(record.key)`. A display rename cannot move or orphan a photo |
| Existing role permissions | `canManageStockPhoto = canAdjustStock` (Owner/Admin/Staff); `canViewStockPhoto = canViewStock` (every active member). Enforced in `Permissions` and in the rules |
| No quantity change, no stock movement | The stock row write carries `lastAction: "photo"` and re-asserts the stored `q` and `min`; **no `/stockMoves` document**, exactly as a note-only save already behaves |
| Safe replacement and removal across two devices | Both documents are written in **one transaction**, guarded by `photoRev`. There is no second storage system to fall out of step with, and nothing to orphan |

## Data model

### On `/stock` — two small fields

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `hasPhoto` | boolean | Whether a photo document exists. Absent means false |
| `photoRev` | number | Increments on every set and replace. The cache key |

Both are tiny and travel with a document the board already reads, so they cost
nothing extra. No image bytes ever touch `/stock` — the collection a Worker
reads, and the one the PWA also reads.

### `/stockPhotos/{stockDocId}` — the bytes

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `key` | string | The logical stock key, for forensics and for a later migration |
| `bytes` | bytes | The WebP image, ≤ 80 KB. **Exempt from indexing** |
| `w`, `h` | number | Pixel dimensions, so the card can lay out before decoding |
| `rev` | number | Matches `/stock.photoRev` |
| `by`, `byUid` | string | Who last set it |
| `at`, `serverAt` | number, timestamp | The repository's existing audit pair |

Firestore's native `bytes` type is used, not base64 in a string: base64 would
inflate the payload by about a third for no benefit.

### The index exemption

`firestore/firestore.indexes.json` currently has an empty `fieldOverrides`
array. It gains one entry:

```json
{
  "collectionGroup": "stockPhotos",
  "fieldPath": "bytes",
  "indexes": []
}
```

An empty `indexes` array disables all automatic indexing for that field. This
must be deployed with `firebase deploy --only firestore:indexes` **before**
any build that writes a photo reaches a device.

## Image quality at 80 KB and 800×800

800×800 is 640,000 pixels. 80 KB across them is about **1.0 bit per pixel**,
which for WebP lossy is roughly comparable to a JPEG at quality 75–80.

- **Identifying an item** — which motor, which box, which shelf — is
  comfortable at this budget.
- **Reading small printed text**, such as a serial or model number on a
  label, is **not reliable**. At 800 px across a whole frame, a label
  occupying a quarter of it has its text rendered in a few dozen pixels and
  lossy compression smears exactly that kind of high-frequency detail.

That matters because photographing a label is an obvious thing to want. The
honest position: this feature identifies items, and the **existing stock note
field** is where a model or serial number should be typed. If label-reading
later turns out to be the real requirement, it needs a larger budget than
Spark comfortably allows, and that is a reason to revisit the plan rather than
to quietly raise the ceiling.

**Encoding:** long edge scaled to at most 800 px, then WebP lossy with the
quality parameter searched downwards until the result is ≤ 80 KB. Quality
floats; the byte ceiling does not. If the floor is reached without fitting —
a pathological image — the save is refused with an explanation rather than
silently storing something worse.

## The write contract

All three operations write **both** documents in **one Firestore
transaction**, through the existing `StockStore` seam extended with photo
reads and writes.

**Set or replace**

1. Scale, rotate and encode locally. Nothing has been written yet.
2. In one transaction: read `/stock` for the stored `q`, `min` and
   `photoRev`; write `/stockPhotos/{id}` with the new bytes and
   `rev = photoRev + 1`; write `/stock` with `hasPhoto: true`,
   `photoRev = rev`, `q` and `min` re-asserted from what was read,
   `lastAction: "photo"`, and the audit metadata.
3. There is no step 3. Nothing was uploaded before the transaction, so
   nothing can be left behind by a failure.

**Remove**

One transaction: delete `/stockPhotos/{id}`, and write `/stock` with
`hasPhoto: false`, `photoRev` incremented, `q` and `min` re-asserted and
`lastAction: "photo"`.

**Two devices at once.** Firestore serialises the transactions and re-runs the
loser's body against the winner's data. The loser reads the winner's
`photoRev` and writes one higher, so both documents stay consistent and the
last writer wins deterministically. A replace racing a remove resolves the
same way.

**This is strictly simpler than the withdrawn Cloud Storage design**, which
needed upload-then-swap-then-delete and could leave orphaned objects when a
delete failed. Here the document *is* the photo: there is no second system, no
orphan, and no cleanup to get wrong. That is a real advantage of the Spark
design, not a consolation.

**What a photo write must never do:** write a `/stockMoves` document; change
`q`, `min` or `off`; or touch `pinned`. Each is asserted in `StockPhotoTest`.

## Access rules

### What is enforceable

```
match /stockPhotos/{stockDoc} {
  allow read:   if member();
  allow create, update: if stockWriter()
                && request.resource.data.bytes is bytes
                && request.resource.data.bytes.size() <= 81920
                && request.resource.data.key is string
                && request.resource.data.rev is number
                && request.resource.data.byUid == mine()
                && !request.resource.data.keys().hasAny(['dealer','contractor','client','gst']);
  allow delete: if stockWriter();
}
```

Rules support the `bytes` type and its `size()`, so the **80 KB ceiling is
enforced on the server**, not merely promised by the client. The price-field
guard mirrors `noPriceData()` on `/stock`: `/stockPhotos` is another
Worker-readable collection, and the same rule should hold there by
construction rather than by memory.

On `/stock`, `'photo'` joins the `lastAction` list —
`['in','out','min','add','pin','note','photo']` — and `'photo'` is
deliberately **not** added to the `/stockMoves` action list: nothing moved.

### What is not enforceable

**Dimensions.** Rules cannot decode an image, so 800×800 is a client-side
constraint. A modified client could store an 80 KB image of any shape. The
consequence is cosmetic rather than dangerous — a badly-shaped image renders
badly — and the byte ceiling still bounds cost.

**Image content.** Nothing verifies that the bytes are a WebP at all, beyond
their size. `contentType` does not exist for a Firestore field. The client
validates the magic bytes on read and shows a placeholder rather than
crashing.

## Spark quotas, usage, and what happens when they run out

### The allowances

| Allowance | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Document reads | **50,000 per day** | [Firebase pricing plans](https://firebase.google.com/docs/projects/billing/firebase-pricing-plans) |
| Document writes | **20,000 per day** | as above |
| Network egress | **10 GB per month** | [Firestore pricing](https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/pricing) |
| Stored data | An allowance exists; **the exact figure is not quoted here** | See note below |

Daily quotas reset at **around midnight Pacific time**. For this business that
is roughly **12:30–13:30 IST — the middle of the working day**. An exhausted
quota therefore spoils the morning and recovers after lunch, which is a worse
shape than it first sounds.

**A note on figures I did not verify.** `firebase.google.com` and
`docs.cloud.google.com` are blocked by this environment's egress proxy, so
these came from search results over the official documentation rather than
from reading the pages. The reads, writes and egress figures were returned
consistently; the **stored-data allowance and the daily delete allowance were
not**, and are deliberately left blank rather than guessed. **The Owner should
read the current table off `firebase.google.com/pricing` before approving.**
Quotas change — that is the point of not writing a number here I cannot see.

### Usage, with the assumptions written down

| Assumption | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | 200 stock rows carry a photo | The Owner photographs what is on the shelf, not the whole 403-product catalogue. Worst case of 403 also shown |
| A2 | 60 KB average, 80 KB ceiling | Quality floats down to fit the ceiling; most photos land under it |
| A3 | 8 active devices | Owner, two Admin/Staff, five Workers |
| A4 | 30 distinct photos opened per device per day | A working day's worth of looking things up |
| A5 | 10 photo changes per day at peak | New stock arriving |
| A6 | Cache-first reads; a photo is fetched once per device and served locally after | The design's central quota assumption. **If this is wrong, every figure below is wrong** |

| Measure | Steady state | Worst case | Against the allowance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stored | 200 × 60 KB ≈ **12 MB** | 403 × 80 KB ≈ **32 MB** | Small either way |
| Reads/day | 30 × 8 = **240** | Cold start, one device fetching all: **403** | 0.5–0.8% of 50,000 |
| Writes/day | 10 × 2 docs = **20** | — | 0.1% of 20,000 |
| Egress/month | 240 × 60 KB × 30 ≈ **430 MB** | Every device fetches every photo once a month: 8 × 32 MB ≈ **258 MB** | 2.6–4.3% of 10 GB |

**The pathological case, named on purpose.** If the on-device cache is cleared
daily — app data wiped, reinstalls, or cache eviction under memory pressure —
eight devices re-fetching 32 MB every day is **7.7 GB per month, about 77% of
the egress allowance**, on photos alone. This is the scenario that turns a
comfortable feature into an outage, and it is why A6 is listed as the
assumption everything rests on.

**Photos are unlikely to be what exhausts the quota. The risk is that they
share it.** Nothing here promises free operation: Spark quotas are finite and
daily, and this feature spends from the same purse as the rest of the app.

### When a quota is exhausted

Requests fail with a resource-exhausted error until the daily reset. On Spark
there is no billing account, so **nothing is charged and nothing
auto-upgrades** — the app simply stops reading or writing. The failure is
**app-wide, not photo-only**: stock, products and quotations all stop too.

The app must therefore degrade honestly rather than confusingly:

- A photo that cannot be fetched shows a placeholder, never a spinner that
  never ends and never a crash.
- A photo write that is refused reports the refusal and leaves the existing
  photo alone — the same posture the stock write already takes.
- The existing error mapping gets a case for resource-exhaustion, so the
  message names the real cause instead of "could not save".

## Migrating to Cloud Storage later

If SMARTIE ever moves to Blaze, this design should not have to be unpicked.
Three things make that migration cheap, and they are worth building in now:

1. **The pointer is already indirect.** Cards read `hasPhoto` and `photoRev`
   from `/stock`; only the photo layer knows where bytes come from. Adding a
   `storagePath` field to `/stockPhotos` changes one layer.
2. **Dual-read for one release.** Prefer `storagePath` when present, fall back
   to `bytes`. Both can coexist per document, so migration is per-item and
   interruptible.
3. **Backfill is a script, not a migration event.** Read each photo document,
   upload the bytes, write `storagePath`, clear `bytes`. It can run against
   staging with the app live, and `key` is on the document so nothing has to
   be joined back to `/stock`.

The one thing that would make this expensive is letting image bytes leak into
`/stock`. The design forbids that for quota reasons; it also keeps the exit
open.

## Android integration

| Piece | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Camera | `ActivityResultContracts.TakePicture()` into a `FileProvider` URI under `cacheDir/stock-photos/` | No `CAMERA` permission, and the provider already exists |
| Gallery | `ActivityResultContracts.PickVisualMedia()` | The system photo picker; no storage permission at any API level |
| Manifest | **Unchanged** | Declaring `CAMERA` would make it a runtime permission the flow then has to request |
| Orientation | `androidx.exifinterface` | `ImageDecoder` handles EXIF itself but is API 28+; `minSdk` here is 23 |
| Encoding | WebP lossy, quality searched down to fit 80 KB | Lossy WebP is supported from API 14, well below this app's `minSdk` of 23 ([supported media formats](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/media/media-formats)). The `WEBP_LOSSY` constant arrived at API 30 and the older `WEBP` constant is deprecated there, so the encoder branches on build version — confirm the exact constant behaviour when implementing |
| Display | Decode from the document's bytes, cached in memory keyed on `key + rev` | No image-loading library is needed for bytes already in hand. Coil stays unused |
| Upload | **No new Firebase dependency** | Firestore is already a dependency. Nothing else is added |

The pipeline's decisions — target long edge, the quality search, the sample
size for a given source size, the rotation implied by each EXIF orientation
value, and the ≤ 80 KB verdict — live in a pure `domain/StockPhoto.kt` and are
unit-tested without Android. Only the bitmap decode/encode touches the
platform. Same split that made the rest of N3 testable.

Per the settled rule, every dialog body is an internal panel the tests drive
directly, and the `AlertDialog` is a wrapper holding no logic. The preview
sheet holds an unconfirmed choice, so it uses the non-dismissible properties
Add stock uses.

## Test plan

**Pure (JVM):** `StockPhotoTest` — the document id for a catalogue key and a
manual key, and that a display rename does not change it; the long edge is
capped at 800; the quality search lands ≤ 80 KB and refuses rather than
exceeding it; the rotation for each EXIF orientation value; a non-WebP byte
sequence is rejected; `hasPhoto` false and `photoRev` 0 read as "no photo".

**Write path:** `StockPhotoWriteTest`, against the existing fake store — a set
writes both documents in one transaction with matching `rev`; `q` and `min`
come from the stored values unchanged; `lastAction` is `"photo"`; **no
movement is written**; a remove deletes the photo document and sets
`hasPhoto: false`; a replayed transaction body increments `rev` exactly once;
a second writer re-running against the winner's data lands one revision higher
with both documents consistent; a replace racing a remove leaves a consistent
pair either way.

**Rules (emulator), `photos.test.js`:** a Worker may read a photo document and
may not write one; Staff may write; an 80 KB payload is accepted and an 80 KB
+ 1 payload is refused; a non-`bytes` field is refused; a document carrying a
price field is refused; `byUid` spoofing is refused; a signed-out caller gets
nothing; `'photo'` is accepted as a `/stock` `lastAction` and refused as a
`/stockMoves` action.

**Compose (Robolectric):** the source sheet offers exactly two options; the
preview writes nothing until confirmed; cancel writes nothing; a Worker sees a
thumbnail and larger view but no add, replace or remove; offline every control
is disabled and says so; a row with `hasPhoto: false` shows no placeholder and
**issues no read**; a photo that fails to load shows a placeholder rather than
an endless spinner.

**Manual, on staging** — these add to the T-S rows; the N3 rows still stand:

is the single most important manual row here: if it fails, the usage
calculation above does not hold.

## Open decisions

1. **Thumbnail on the card, or an icon?** The plan assumes a thumbnail, lazily
   fetched. An icon instead — bytes fetched only when the larger view is
   opened — costs almost no reads at all. Recommendation: thumbnails, with the
   icon as the fallback if T-P14 or the console shows read pressure.
2. **What happens at 200 photos and growing.** Nothing breaks, but the cache
   competition in limitation 4 grows. Revisit if the shelf list grows past a
   few hundred photographed rows.
3. **Whether label legibility is actually required.** If it is, this ceiling
   does not deliver it and the feature needs rethinking rather than retuning.
4. **The stored-data allowance figure**, to be read off the pricing page by
   the Owner rather than assumed here.

## When I would recommend postponing photos instead

Not now, but the conditions are worth writing down so the answer is not
re-litigated from memory:

- If the team grows well beyond the eight devices in A3, or the board is
  opened far more often than A4 assumes, photos start competing seriously for
  the shared quota.
- If T-P14 fails and cache-first cannot be made to hold, the egress figures
  become the pathological case rather than the steady state.
- If label legibility turns out to be the real requirement.

Any of those means the feature wants Cloud Storage, and Cloud Storage means
Blaze. In that case postponing photos is better than shipping a version that
quietly degrades the rest of the app.

## Out of scope for N3.1

Multiple photos per item, photos on products rather than stock rows, photos in
quotations or the PDF, annotation or cropping, barcode scanning, and any
production deployment. Purchase photo attachments belong with N4 if they are
wanted at all. N3.1 changes no N3 quantity, archive or permission behaviour,
and must not become the reason N3's outstanding device checks slip.
