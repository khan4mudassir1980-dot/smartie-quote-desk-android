# N4.4 — what the run #113 phone pass found

The plan of record for N4.4. `docs/PROJECT-STATUS.md` remains the single
statement of what is true now; this file says what this batch set out to do and
why each decision was taken.

## The standing change to how batches are tested

**There is no phone testing between batches any more.** Every phone row is run
once, at the end, against one staging APK and one staging rules deployment.
CI green stays mandatory on every push.

That raises what CI has to carry. A defect CI does not catch now survives until
the single final pass, so this batch spends most of its test budget on the one
thing the suite provably could not see — see B1.

## What the pass passed

T-D1, T-D2, T-D9 (the Owner half), T-D10, T-D12, T-D13, T-D14, T-D15, T-D16.

Everything else stays owed and is listed in `docs/PHONE-TEST-CHECKLIST.md`,
which from this batch on is the cumulative record of what a phone still has to
prove.

## B1 — only three row actions ever appeared

**Reported:** on every open card, for every role, only Edit, Urgency and
Received showed. Remove was missing on the Owner's own untouched row and on a
Staff account's own untouched row; Close-short was missing on the Owner's
partly received row. Each card had a large blank area under the buttons.

**Root cause.** `SmartieCard` fixed the card's height with
`Modifier.height(IntrinsicSize.Min)` — needed for one reason only, so that a
3dp accent bar could `fillMaxHeight()` — and then clipped the card to its
rounded corners. The row actions are a `FlowRow`, which at 360dp wraps: Edit,
Urgency and Received take about 241dp of the 303dp a card offers, so **Remove
and Close-short are always on the second line**. Where the intrinsic height and
the real measurement disagree, that second line falls outside the fixed height
and the clip erases it. The same disagreement in the other direction is the
blank area. One cause, both symptoms, and it explains why the missing controls
were always the last ones in the row rather than a particular role's.

**Why the suite did not catch it.** `assertIsDisplayed()` asks a node about its
own bounds; it does not ask whether an ancestor painted it. A clipped control
is still "displayed" by that definition. Two tests looked like they covered
this and did not: `PurchaseSmallPhoneScreenTest`'s comment says "Four controls
on one card" while its loop covers three, and `PurchaseCardLayoutScreenTest`
measures against a test host `Box` rather than the card.

**The fix.** The accent bar is painted with `drawBehind` instead of being a
`fillMaxHeight` sibling, so no intrinsic pass exists to disagree with the real
one and the card is plain wrap-content. Fixed once in the shared component
rather than in the purchase card, by the Owner's decision: stock, products and
team cards carry the same latent defect, and two implementations would drift.

**The test that would have caught it** asserts a node's clipped bounds equal
its unclipped bounds, that they lie inside the card's own bounds, that it
measures at least 48dp, and that it is clickable — run at 360dp for every card
family with the longest footer each can show, plus an assertion that the gap
below the footer is the card's padding and no more.

## B2 — one requirement rendered twice

**Reported:** two identical "yysh" cards on the Owner's open list, same author
and date; the Open header read 8 against 9 visible cards.

**Root cause.** `PurchaseWriteRepository.create()` mints a fresh document id on
every invocation. The replay guarantee the file documents covers Firestore
re-running one transaction body; it does nothing across two calls. The Add
sheet is dismissed only on success, so after an ambiguous failure — the commit
landed, the acknowledgement did not — the sheet stays open with the typing
intact and invites a second Add, which writes **a second document**. Same name,
same author, timestamps seconds apart, one rendered date. That the two ids
differ is exactly why the `LazyColumn` rendered both instead of throwing on a
duplicate key.

**The fix.** The requirement's id is minted once per open Add sheet and reused
on a retry, so the second attempt lands on the same document and is refused as
one already added rather than becoming a twin. `PurchaseRecord.id` is taken
from the Firestore document id rather than a stored `id` field, and the
requirements flow is de-duplicated by that id, so the list and any count of it
come from one source that cannot disagree with itself.

**The count is not explained, and is not dismissed.** All nine cards, both
"yysh" among them, were above the "Received and closed" heading — inside Open.
On the code as written that cannot happen: the header is `active.size` and
`items(...)` iterates that same list. The header may have read 9 at that
resolution. Rather than argue it, the batch adds a test that the Open header's
number equals the number of open cards rendered, which either finds the
mechanism or stands as a guard.

**The two existing rows are two real documents.** Nothing in this batch merges
them; the fix stops new ones. Both will still show, and the Owner removes one
by hand.

## The changes

**C1 — one collapsed History on the Purchase screen.** The always-open
"Received and closed" section becomes a collapsed **History** at the bottom of
the list, holding Received and a nested collapsed Removed, reusing the pattern
`PurchaseHistoryScreen` already uses. Its role filtering is
`PurchaseHistory.received/removed`, the same app-level filter, which is what
stops a Staff account seeing another person's received row on the main list —
a break of the Owner's standing decision that Staff sees only what it raised.
`More → Purchase history` shows the same data from the same functions.

**C2–C5, C7 — the card.** Tighter padding; the quantity line bold and larger,
at two lines so `10 required · 4 received · 6 remaining` cannot ellipsize at
360dp; the note in a lightly tinted box; the trailing "N in" no longer hidden
on a removed row that received something.

**C5's layout, as proposed and approved:** at most **three actions inline, then
an overflow `⋯`**, in a fixed order so every role sees the same shape — 
Received, Edit and Urgency inline; Close-short, Remove and Reopen in the
overflow. Touch targets stay 48dp, including the menu items. At 360dp three
buttons and the overflow measure about 297dp against 303dp available, so it is
one row and does not wrap. The overflow has room for the full wording, so
**"Close with N received"** appears there rather than the card's cramped
"Close short" — which is where the phone pass expected to find it.

**C6 — a person's role beside their name.** Resolved **by uid** (`byUid`,
`rcvUid`, `deletedBy`), never by display name. Where the uid cannot be
resolved, the name shows alone.

The Owner's decision, recorded because the limit is not obvious: another
person's `/users` document is readable only by an Owner or Administrator
(`firestore.rules`, `allow read: if (signedIn() && mine() == uid) || admin()`),
so a Manager or Staff phone cannot look anyone up. The role suffix therefore
appears on Owner and Administrator phones and nowhere else, and the members
listener is gated to those roles so a Manager or Staff account never attaches
one the rules would refuse. No rules change in this batch.

**Deferred, by the Owner's decision:** a write-time role snapshot
(`byRole` / `rcvRole` / `delRole`), validated in the rules against the writer's
actual role, so every role sees who did what. That needs a rules change and is
scheduled with a later rules batch.

## Scope

UI and app state only. **No `firestore.rules` change, no index change, nothing
deployed, production neither read nor written.** The two items that might have
needed a rule — history filtering on the main list, and role resolution — are
settled above without one.
